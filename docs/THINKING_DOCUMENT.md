# Account Quest — Thinking Document

**What it does.** Account Quest is a Customer Success CRM that retrieves live account data from the Questathon MCP server and turns it into decisions. For each account it answers the five questions: what the book is worth, who holds the power, what to do first, what is about to go wrong, and what we still do not know. It also tracks live events and writes notes back to the game.

**Who it is for.** A customer success manager who owns a small book of accounts and must decide, quickly and defensibly, where the next hour of effort goes. A judge or manager should be able to open one page and see the answer and its proof.

**How it makes decisions.** A deterministic rules engine ranks candidate moves by deal value (up to 40 points), deadline urgency (up to 35), an open blocker (15), renewal status (10) and a win probability only if the data states one. It also generates moves for power holders who are guarded or not yet met. The top three per account get an owner and a due date. The owner defaults to the account CSM unless the data names one. If the customer has no usable deadline, the due date is a clearly labelled internal target, never presented as the customer's date. Risks are red, amber or green by explicit rules (overdue or ≤7-day deadlines, open blockers, guarded decision makers, renewals not yet agreed), and every classification prints the evidence behind it.

**Evidence and source IDs.** Every record gets an ID that points at something that really exists (`DEAL:<account>:<id>`, `DOC:<id>`, `PERSON:…`, `SIG:…`). Clicking an ID shows the raw stored MCP record. Deal values are marked *Verified* only if a paperwork record is cited or the same amount appears in an account document; *Told* if a person or signal said it; *Unverified* if it exists only on the account sheet. Unverified amounts are shown separately from verified totals, and a document that quotes a different amount raises a contradiction flag. Raw MCP responses are stored next to the normalized view.

**What it does not know yet.** Anything the data does not state stays *Unknown* and appears in Q5 with why it matters, who could tell us and the next question to ask: missing decision makers or budget owners, unverified or missing values, unconfirmed deadlines, unmet stakeholders, missing technical documents, unknown renewal terms. The system never guesses a probability, a stakeholder role or a date.

**Live updates.** `get_signals` is fetched on request. A signal not seen before is flagged NEW, the affected account's insights are marked for review with a banner, and the cached account sheet can be refreshed when the 60-second MCP limit allows. Notes go through `write_note` and are shown as saved only when the MCP server confirms success; failures are shown as failures.

**How AI was used.** No external model produces customer facts. The "AI-assisted" layer is a rules engine whose outputs are labelled recommendations, not verified facts. AI coding assistance (Claude) was used to build the application; the analysis itself is reproducible and inspectable.

**Limitations and next steps.** The MCP payload is read through field-alias heuristics and should be checked against the real schema after the first sync. Contradiction detection covers value conflicts and stage-vs-blocker conflicts only. With more time: LLM-assisted summaries gated behind evidence IDs, a per-stakeholder statement timeline to detect contradictory claims, an editable scoring model, multi-user permissions, and automatic account refresh after high-impact signals.
